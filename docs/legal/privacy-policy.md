# Professor Pine: Privacy Policy

_Last updated: November 14, 2026_

Professor Pine ("Pine") is a Discord app that helps a community organize Pokémon GO raids, max battles, raid trains and meetups, with a web map beside it. This policy covers the Pine application run by its owner, Googer, and the web site that belongs to it. Pine is open source, and other people run their own copies; a copy run by somebody else keeps its own data and is that person's responsibility.

## In short

- Pine keeps what it needs to do what you ask of it: your Discord id, the name you show, what you sign up for, what you ask to be told about, and the settings you choose.
- Pine does not sell your data, show ads, or use analytics or tracking.
- Pine does not read your messages. It reads a message only when you mention it, write to it directly, post in a channel that is set aside for it, or when a member points it at a message from the Apps menu.
- Your name is off the long-term statistics unless you switch that on.
- One Pine can serve several Discord servers. Each server's data is its own: another server's members and staff do not see it, but for the few gyms that servers had in common before this version, and the outlines of a server's regions, which the staff of servers nearby see while they edit their own regions (below).
- Some things stay until you remove them or ask for them to be removed. Leaving a server does not remove them, because Pine cannot see who leaves. When a server removes Pine, that server's data is deleted about 30 days later.

## Servers, their staff and Pine's operators

**Servers.** Pine works in the Discord servers that have added it and that this Pine allows. Which servers may have it is decided by Pine's operators (below): the servers it was already in when this version first started (on a new Pine that anybody could add to a server at that moment, only those whose Discord owner is Pine's owner or one of its operators), those they list or allow, or, on a Pine open to every server, any server they have not blocked. A server it does not allow is told so, where Pine may post there, and left.

**A server's staff** (its owner, members with Manage Server, and its admins, moderators and bot developers) set Pine up in their server and decide how it is used there: its channels, its regions on the map, its gyms, which features are on, and whether visitors who are not logged in may see the server's web map. They answer for what they set up. Until they have finished setting Pine up, the server is "in setup": Pine tells its members only that it is not set up yet, and posts, sends and reports nothing there.

**Pine's operators** are its owner and the people the owner names as operators. They look after what every server shares (the list of raid bosses and its corrections, event times, the map's picture cache), and they alone see which servers Pine is in, allow or block a server, make Pine leave one, and remove a member's data on request. On a Pine that is not open to every server, the admins, moderators and bot developers of the servers it allows may also change what every server shares, from their own server; the owner is told of every such change, with who made it and from which server.

**What is a server's and what is yours.** A server's own data is its settings, regions, gyms and their descriptions, PokéStops and Power Spots, parties, history, boards, saved routes, what its members asked to be told about there, the choices its members made for that server alone (whether they are named in its history and on its boards, whether they are told of its new trains, and their request for their old-bot history there), and its moderation and meetup records. A gym that several servers already had in common when this version first started stays one gym, with one name and one description: what either server's staff change about it, both servers show. The outlines of a server's regions are shown to the staff of the other servers this Pine serves whose regions lie near them (within about 2 km of the area they span), while those staff edit their own regions on the web, so that neighbors' regions can meet without overlapping: the outline alone, without its name, its channel or which server it is. The outlines of a server Pine has left, of one whose data is being deleted and of one still in setup are not shown to other servers' staff. Yours, kept once for every server, is your row with your Discord id, your friend code, in-game name, Pokebattler id and the settings that hold in every server, your web logins, your request for Pine's Wayspot export tool if you made one, and the record that Pine has written to you (under "Direct messages").

## What Pine keeps about you

**Who you are on Discord.** Your Discord user id, and the name and team you show in the server when you use Pine. Pine has no access to your e-mail address, password, friends or other servers.

**What you tell Pine about yourself.** If you set them: your friend code, your in-game name, your Pokebattler id, and your settings (which pings and direct messages you want, whether your name goes on your reports, and the like). These are kept once for you, not per server, but for three that you set in each server apart: whether you are named in its history, whether you are on its boards, and whether you are told of its new trains. Those three are that server's.

**What you ask to be told about.** The Pokémon, raid tiers, research rewards, Team GO Rocket targets and gyms you want notices for, per server.

**What you take part in.** While a raid, max battle, train or meetup is on, Pine keeps who signed up, with name, team, status and extra players, and shows it in the post. When it ends, the live record is deleted and a history record is kept for statistics. That record holds what happened (teams, counts, times) but **not who you are, unless you switched on "history" in `/settings`**. The same goes for the leaderboards: you are named there only if you switched them on. Switching history off again takes your id out of every record of that server.

**What you report.** Field research, Team GO Rocket and wild spawn reports carry your name while they are shown, and are deleted when they end (at midnight, or after about half an hour for a grunt or a spawn). Long-term counts of such reports carry your id only with history switched on, and never the exact place.

**Screenshots.** When Pine reads a screenshot, it keeps the whole picture for a day, or until a day after its raid ends. If the reading was refused or doubtful, or somebody gave feedback on it, a copy is kept for up to 30 days so that the reader can be improved. The picture is the whole image you posted, including anything else that was on your screen. It can be seen by you, by the member who had Pine read it if that was somebody else, by the server's staff, and by Pine's owner.

**Reports to moderators and meetup reviews.** If you report a message or a party to the moderators, Pine keeps your report and, for a message, a copy of that message (its author, its text and the names of its attachments). If you ask for a meetup that needs approval, appeal a block, or are blocked from making meetups, Pine keeps that record with the names of those involved. These records are for the server's moderators and have no time limit.

**Who did what.** For 30 days Pine keeps a log, for the server's staff, of who made, changed or ended a party. This includes who made a report that is shown as anonymous: "anonymous" hides your name from other members, not from staff.

**The web site.** Logging in with Discord tells Pine your Discord id and the name you show, and nothing else; Pine asks Discord for the smallest permission there is (`identify`) and does not keep the access token. A login is remembered for 30 days in a cookie (`pine_session`). A second, short-lived cookie is used only during the login itself. There are no other cookies. Your browser's local storage holds your map preferences and unsaved edits; they never leave your browser. Pine does not store your IP address; it holds it in memory for about a minute to limit how fast one address can ask.

**If you used the earlier Pine.** The records of raids organized with the earlier version of the bot were brought along without member ids. Your id is attached to your own old raids only if you choose "Add my old-bot history" in `/settings`, and is taken off again when you switch history off.

## When Pine reads messages

Pine does not receive the text of messages in a server. It reads a message only when:

- the message mentions Pine (for example a raid screenshot posted with a mention);
- you write to Pine in a direct message;
- the message is posted in a region's board, a channel set aside for Pine, where it reads screenshots and then removes the message;
- a member chooses "Report to moderators" or "Read screenshot" on the message in the Apps menu. Another member can do this with a message you posted; Discord then hands Pine that one message.

## What other people can see

- **Everyone who can see a post** sees who signed up, with name, team and status. Posts stay on Discord after a party ends, as any Discord message does.
- **Members of servers you share with Pine** can look up your friend code and find you by your in-game name, if you set them.
- **Staff of the server** can see your settings and what you asked to be told about, the 30-day logs of who did what and who changed the server's settings, kept screenshots, and the moderation and meetup records. Staff of another server see none of this server's.
- **Pine's operators** see which servers Pine is in, with each server's name, member count, whether it is set up and when it last had a party, but never its members. When they look up a member to remove their data, they see how many records Pine holds of that member, not what the records say. Pine's owner runs the computer Pine runs on, and so can see everything Pine stores.
- **Everyone** sees the leaderboards and anything you post with a "Share" button. You are on a leaderboard only if you switched that on.
- **Visitors of the web map who are not logged in** see a server's regions and gyms only, and only where the server's staff leave its map open to them: on a Pine open to every server a server's map is closed to them until its staff open it, and a closed one is not there for them at all. Somebody logged in who is not a member of the server sees the same regions and gyms. Nothing about members.

## Direct messages

Pine sends you a direct message when something you asked to be told about is reported, and you can stop each kind from the message itself or in `/settings`. It also tells you when a party you signed up for changes its time or place, and about the outcome of your own meetup requests, appeals and screenshots; these cannot be switched off, because they are about something you are part of.

Discord limits how many people a bot may write to for the first time, for all its servers together. So that one busy server cannot use that up for the others, each server may start only so many first direct messages at a time, and Pine keeps a record of each member it has written to: your Discord id and when Pine first wrote to you, nothing else. The record is yours, not a server's; it stays until your data is removed on request (then your next direct message counts as a first one again).

## Who else receives data

Pine runs on its owner's own computer. A few other services are involved:

- **Discord**, through which you use Pine.
- **Cloudflare**, which carries the web site's traffic and so sees visitors' IP addresses.
- **Map and picture hosts.** Your browser loads map tiles (Stadia Maps, OpenStreetMap) and game pictures (Leek Duck, GitHub) directly from their hosts, which see your IP address as any web site does.
- **Google Maps** and **OpenStreetMap's search** receive the places Pine looks up: the coordinates of gyms, or a place name typed into `/find-region`. Nothing about who asked.
- **Pokebattler** receives your Pokebattler id when you ask `/counters` to use your own Pokémon, and only then.
- **GitHub.** Where this Pine sends a server's gym requests to GitHub (only for a server it allows, on a Pine that is not open to every server), `/request` files a public request for a gym; your name, your server's name and what you wrote go with it, as the command says. Any other server's requests go to its own moderators on Discord.

Pine does not share your data with anybody else, and never for advertising.

**A server's share of the operator's services.** Google's lookups cost the owner, who gives Pine a monthly budget; the servers share it by how much each still needs, and the operators may raise a server's share. For that, Pine counts each server's lookups per day and keeps the raises of its share for the month: counts by server, nothing about members. Each server's first direct messages are shared out as "Direct messages" says.

## How long things are kept

| What | How long |
|---|---|
| Live parties and reports | Until they end |
| History and counts without your id | Indefinitely |
| Your id in the history and on the boards | While you have them switched on |
| Your choices for one server (history, boards, new trains) | Until you change them, or the server's data is deleted |
| Wants, gym targets, saved routes, friend code, in-game name, settings | Until you remove them |
| The record that Pine has written to you (your id and the time of the first message) | Until your data is removed on request |
| Kept screenshots | 1 day; up to 30 days when kept for review |
| The staff's logs of who did what and of changes to the server's settings | 30 days |
| Reports to moderators, meetup requests, blocks and appeals | No time limit |
| A server's counts of Google lookups and the raises of its share | Until the server's data is deleted |
| Everything that is a server's, once Pine is removed from it | About 30 days, then deleted at Pine's next daily cleanup |
| The record of a server (when it was recorded, allowed, blocked or left, and which operator allowed or blocked it) | No time limit |
| Places imported from Wayfarer before version 2.1 (a Pine upgraded from 2.0) | Until a later cleanup removes them |
| The earlier bot's index of old raids by member (a Pine moved from it) | Until the owner removes it |
| Web logins | 30 days after last use, or until you log out |
| Backups of the database | 90 days |
| Server logs (which can hold Discord ids, never message text) | A few days to weeks, until they roll over |

The times above hold while a server has Pine: everything that is a server's is deleted about 30 days after Pine is removed from it, with the exceptions below.

## When a server removes Pine

When Pine is removed from a server, or an operator makes it leave one, the server is marked as left, and nothing is deleted yet: its parties are left as they are, and nothing is posted, sent, swept, redrawn or looked up for it. If Pine is added back before its data is deleted, it picks up where it left off.

About 30 days after Pine left it, at Pine's next daily cleanup, and never while Pine is in it again, everything that is the server's is deleted (but for what "What stays" below names): its settings, regions, its links to gyms (a gym no other server has is deleted with its descriptions; one that another server has stays, as that server's), its PokéStops and Power Spots, parties, history, boards, saved routes, what its members asked to be told about there, its members' choices for it (history, boards, new trains, old-bot history requests), its Pokémon nicknames, its reports at PokéStops and their counts, its Wayfarer imports and their review, its moderation and meetup records, its staff's logs, its counts of Google lookups, and the screenshots kept from it (any that could not be removed are tried again at each daily cleanup). If Pine is added back while this is under way, Pine tells the server's members that it is getting the server ready until the deletion is over, and then the server starts anew, in setup. Added back once its data is deleted, the server starts anew, in setup, too.

What stays: the record of the server itself (its Discord id; when it was recorded, allowed, blocked or left, and when its data was deleted; and which operator allowed or blocked it, by their Discord id), so that an operator's decision about a server outlasts its data; your own data that is not any server's (your row with your settings, your web logins, your request for the Wayspot export tool, the record that Pine has written to you); the ids of some of Pine's own messages in the server's channels (copies of an ended party's post in a channel that is no region's, such as a remote or elite raid channel), which say nothing about members and may stay until a later cleanup removes them, since nothing in them says which server they are of; and backups made before the deletion, until they age out (90 days).

Two more things stay on a Pine that came from an earlier version. On a Pine upgraded from version 2.0, the places it imported from Wayfarer before the upgrade (PokéStops and gyms, with their addresses; nothing about members) stay until a later cleanup removes them: nothing in them says which server they were for. On a Pine moved from the earlier bot, that bot's index of its old raids by member (Discord ids with the raids each took part in), which Pine only reads to add your old-bot history and cannot change, stays on the owner's computer until the owner removes the server's part of it; Pine tells the owner when a deleted server has one.

An operator may make Pine leave a server, and may block a server so that it cannot add Pine again.

## Your choices

- **See your data:** `/settings` shows what Pine has for you and lets you change it.
- **Stop notices:** `/unwant`, `/unwant-all`, `/untarget-all`, or the buttons on a direct message.
- **Remove what you set:** `/friend-code remove`, `/nickname remove`, an empty Pokebattler id in `/settings`.
- **Take your name off the statistics:** switch history and boards off in `/settings`.
- **Keep your name off your reports:** "Name on reports and trains" in `/settings`.
- **Log out of the web site:** the menu on the map.

**Leaving a server does not delete anything**, because Pine cannot see who leaves. And some things you cannot remove yourself: your basic row with your Discord id, the reports you made to moderators, and your meetup records.

**Having your data removed.** Ask (the end of this page says how), and Pine's owner (or one of its operators) removes your data in every server at once. That takes away your settings, friend code and in-game name, everything you asked to be told about, your saved routes, your web logins, your request for the Wayspot export tool, the record that Pine has written to you, the screenshots Pine kept from you, and your id everywhere in the history and on the boards, together with the names and descriptions of the trains and meetups you made while your history was switched on (a record made without it does not say who made it, so Pine cannot find it). On the reports you made to moderators, and on your finished meetup requests, appeals and questions, who you are is removed; what you wrote stays as the moderators' record, without your name or id.

What stays: a block from making meetups and the moderators' records about you, which are kept for the safety of others; anything of yours the moderators have not decided yet, and a meetup of yours that is still going on, which go when the removal is run again afterwards; what you did as a member of a server's staff (a decision, a ruling, a block you gave), which keeps your name, and as an operator (a server you allowed or blocked), which keeps your id; the staff's 30-day log; and what is going on right now, which ends by itself. Pine does not edit messages on Discord that name you. Backups made before the removal age out within 90 days.

## Children

Pine is for people who may use Discord under Discord's own rules for age. Pine does not ask for or keep anybody's age.

## Changes and contact

This policy may change; the date at the top says when it last did.

For a question or a request about your data, message Pine's owner, Googer, on Discord, or ask the moderators of your server to pass it on. Please do not put personal details in a public GitHub issue.
