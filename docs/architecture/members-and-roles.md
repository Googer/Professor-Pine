# Members and roles

What the bot knows of members without a member list: their settings and rows, the removal of a member's data on request, one member looked up, who of many can see a channel, and self-assigned roles with their menus. Read before changing member settings, member lookups, `/admin forget` or `/iam`, `/iamnot` and `/admin self-roles`, and before adding a table or a file that holds a member's id or name.

## Settings and rows

- Member settings are read through `ctx.users` (`get`, `getMany`, `update`, `withNickname`). Whoever is about to ping several members reads them in one go with `settingsOf(users, ids, log)` (`src/services/party-change.ts`): a member without a row has the defaults, and a read that fails is logged and pings nobody. Tests use `MemoryUsers` (`test/support/users.ts`).
- `withNickname` (`/nickname find`) reads along `user_nickname_index (nickname)` of migration 050 (plan 8Z-2, audit X4): `User` has a row for every member ever seen and had no index on the name, so each lookup read them all. `User` is a table of the legacy bot: an index added to it changes nothing of what that bot reads or writes.
- Whether a member is named in the party history is per server (`ctx.historyOptIns`, the history of `/settings`; `history.md`), like new trains; it is no column of `User`. So is whether they are ranked on the server's boards (the boards of `/settings`, plan 8H-6: `LeaderboardOptIn`, `isOnBoards`, `joinBoards`, `leaveBoards` of `ctx.historyOptIns`; `history.md`, "The boards"): off by default, switching it on turns the history opt-in on with it where it was off (and says so), and the history's opt-out takes the member off the boards too. The panel of `/settings` and `/admin member-settings` show both; a DM's panel says they are set per server.
- **`/settings`** (plan 8S-5) is one command without options (`SimpleCommand`, `defer: 'late'`, `MEMBER_READ_THROTTLE`; offered in servers and in a DM with the bot): a private components-v2 panel (`src/commands/settings/panel.ts`, one container of `LIST_COLOUR`, 39 components at most of Discord's 40 since plan 8H-10's section of the old bot's history: the next control the panel gains needs a rework of its layout, a second panel or lines that share a component) laid out as the lines of `settingsLines` (`settings-lines.ts`, one function for the panel and `/admin member-settings`, its words neutral for both: "Pings in posts: **on**", "History in this server: **off**", "Name on reports and trains: **Visible**"), each with its control: the four pings share one row of buttons that carry their name and state ("Shouts: on"), the boss DM, new trains, the history and the boards a button beside each (`Turn on` / `Turn off`, the history's `Turn off…`; green, Success, means only "on now": a ping that is on, while `Turn on` is blue, Primary, and what is off or turns off gray, Secondary), the status and the privacy a string select each with the stored option `default`, the Pokebattler id "Change…" (a dialog with the saved id in its line, `openToken`; its Submit checked by the handler's guard, before it counts or is acknowledged, A1: 1 to 2,147,483,647, empty clears it, a Submit without the line stale, A2; opening the dialog counts as a use of `/settings`, in the command's `MEMBER_READ_THROTTLE` window, never in the writes'), the friend code and in-game name read-only with `/friend-code register` and `/nickname register`, and in a server where notifications are on the counts of `/wants`'s lines and of the gyms, each with Show. The old bot's history (plan 8H-10, `history.md`): with the history on, `ctx.legacyIndex` there (`LEGACY_INDEX_DIR` set) and a file of the member in the server's member index (`hasFile`, which reads no file but asks whether the bot may read it), a section of its own, "Your raids, trains and meetups with the old bot in this server can be added to your history." with "Add my old-bot history" (blue; `settings:legacy:add`, a write of the panel's window); once asked, its line in the member's words, one name throughout ("your old-bot history", B7): "Your old-bot history is being added.", "Your old-bot history is added: 1,234 raids, trains and meetups." ("There was none of your old-bot raids, trains or meetups left to add to your history." for none), and a failed one "Your old-bot history could not be added. Ask staff, then press **Try again**." in a section with "Try again" (the same control, A1: it queues the request again; still 39 components), where the index is there with a file of theirs, else "Your old-bot history could not be added: ask staff." alone, a text display; nothing where there is nothing to say, and nothing of a waiting request where the installation has no index any more (B5). These rules are `legacyLine`'s (`panel.ts`), which staff's lines use too. `settingsLines` words it neutrally for staff ("Old-bot history: **not added**", "**being added**", "**added** (1,234 raids, trains and meetups)", "**could not be added** (why)", the worker's words for staff, B8); the panel draws the member's own words from `legacyWords` beside it. A press queues the fill-in (`ctx.legacyFillIns.request`) where the file is there and nothing waits or is done, starts the worker and says "Asked: it takes a few minutes."; where the request waits or is done it says so and asks nothing; with the history off meanwhile, "Your history is off: turn it on first.". Turning the history on, also through the boards (B12), adds " Your old-bot history can be added too: press **Add my old-bot history**." where it can be. The panel reads the member's row first, then the rest at once, the fill-in's row and the file's existence among them (the row's read remembers its id for the list reads, `round-trips.db.test.ts`: 8 reads, 10 for a press). In a DM (`!interaction.inGuild()`) the per-server lines are one: "New trains, your history, the boards, wants and gyms are set per server: use `/settings` in that server." Its old subcommands are gone; a client that still sends one (`formerSubcommands`) opens the panel, its options unread.
- The panel's controls (`settingsControls`, kind `settings`, `custom-id.ts`): every control **sets** a state, never toggles what it reads (`settings:<setting>:set:on|off`, the menus `settings:auto-status:set` and `settings:privacy:set` with the value picked); the member and the server are the interaction's. A press reads what it changes (the member's row, or the write's own answer: `newTrains.set`, `optIn`, `joinBoards`, `leaveBoards`, `optOut` say whether anything changed), writes, and draws the panel again from a fresh read (`panelNow`) with a line of what changed under its title ("Pings for shouts are off.", "That was on already." where it was so: a panel drawn before a change elsewhere), shown in place (`pressAnswer`, `readOrDeferPress`); the slow writes (`joinBoards`, `optOut`) acknowledge with `deferUpdate` first. Show answers apart (`answerApart`) with exactly what `/wants` / `/targets` answer (`wantsAnswer`, `targetsAnswer`), a late read acknowledged with a private `deferReply`, never `deferUpdate`; the panel's counts may go stale after a Remove there (accepted). The server's settings and Show are refused outside a server, Show where notifications are off (`requirePanelServer`, a guard: nothing counts). Turning the history off asks first where it takes something away (`namedIn` > 0 or on the boards, read in time, `SLOW_READ` past it): the dialog `settings:history:forget` (one of the one-shot dialogs of `discord-framework.md`), "Turn your history off", "I will take your Discord id off N entries of your history (the server still counts them) and take you off the boards. Submit turns your history off."; its Submit `deferUpdate`s, opts out and says what the opt-out took away by its own count; with nothing to take away it is off at once. One window for every control but Change… (`MEMBER_WRITE_THROTTLE`), the Submit five in ten minutes in its own (`HISTORY_OFF_THROTTLE`), whose button is refused while that is full (`checks`). Two quick presses may redraw out of order (accepted). Accepted too (review A3, A4): "Turn off…" acts at once where there is nothing to take away (S2), so its `…` promises a question that does not always come, and it is refused while the Submit's window is full even then; a menu pick that is refused (a value it does not know, the window full) leaves the client showing the option picked until the panel is drawn again (S17, S18).
- A repository method that changes one member's rows of a server runs inside `withMemberLocked` (`src/db/repositories/member-rows.ts`): the member's `User` row is locked first, by snowflake, and the rows are read after the lock. The row ids of servers and members are remembered per process in `RowIds` (`row-ids.ts`; one made in `src/app.ts` and given to the repositories of guilds, users, wants and gym targets; `RowIds.none` remembers nothing): a row made inside a transaction is remembered only after its commit, never inside a caller's transaction, and a remembered row that is gone is forgotten and the change run once more (the legacy bot deletes `Guild` rows). So is a member's change that makes their row (`create`) and finds the row it read just before gone once it holds the lock: the owner's `/admin forget` deleted it and committed while the change waited (plan 8G); the change is then made as for a new member (`member-rows.db.test.ts` holds the removal's lock while it waits). `src/commands/round-trips.db.test.ts` pins how many round trips the common commands make.
- `/admin member-settings` is for the server's own staff alone (`requireManagement` on the subcommand: admins, moderators, the owner; a bot developer, whom the rest of `/admin` takes, is refused: plan 8Z-2, the audit's L-D6). It (`memberSettingsLines`) names a member's wants as their own `/wants` names its lines (`wantLineNames` over `wantEntries`, one path: both tables, "Costumed Pikachu", "Tier 5 raids", "Team GO Rocket", the rewards; by `/wants`'s order), the first five and a count, with the unreadable rows of both tables.

## Removing a member's data on request (plan 8G)

Pine's privacy policy promises that what it holds of somebody is removed when they ask (Matt, 2026-11-10). `/admin forget member:<user>` is how the owner does it, across every server.

- **Whoever adds a table, a column or a file that holds a member's id or name adds it here too**: to `MemberDataRepository` (`src/db/repositories/member-data-repository.ts`, the one place that names every such table: `held` counts it, `remove` takes it or the comment says why it stays), to the table below, and to the service's database test, which dumps every table before and after.
- **The command** (`src/commands/admin/forget.ts`): a part of `/admin`, the bot's owner's alone (`requireOwner`, `src/discord/guards.ts`: `OWNER_ID`, no role needed; staff and bot developers get "Only the owner of this Pine can do that."), on the command and again on its controls. The user option takes anybody's id, in the server or not: nothing is asked of Discord, no member is fetched, nobody is told or pinged. `defer: 'ephemeral'` (the look reads every table and both screenshot folders).
- **First a look, then the act.** The command answers privately with what Pine holds of that id (`MemberRemoval.look`: counts, never the data), what a removal takes, what it leaves and why, and what is left for the owner to do by hand (`forgetLookMessage`, `src/render/member-removal.ts`), with "Remove…" (`forget:<token>:ask`) only where there is anything to take (`removable(look)`, the one rule of the button, of "there is nothing to remove" and of a second run). The token is a `PendingRequests` entry of the owner who asked, good for `FORGET_MINUTES` (10) and answered once. "Remove…" reads nothing but that entry and opens the question as a dialog (one of the one-shot dialogs of `discord-framework.md`: "Remove a member’s data", "This cannot be undone. Submit removes it."); only its Submit (`forget:<token>:remove`) removes: it takes the entry, acknowledges privately and calls `MemberRemoval.remove`, then answers with what went, what is left and the hand-work (`forgetOutcomeMessage`). A removal the database refuses is answered with the step that failed (`forgetFailedMessage`): nothing changed.
- **One service** (`MemberRemoval`, `src/services/member-removal.ts`, `ctx.memberRemoval`): `remove(memberId, by)` is the only way it goes. The database first, in one transaction (`MemberData.remove`: `MemberDataRepository` runs the owning repositories' methods over one `trx`, each of which joins it, in the order of the table below (`REMOVAL_STEPS`: the member's row first, as their own changes lock it; then the history, in the opt-out's order); a deadlock runs all of it again; a failure rolls all of it back and is thrown as `MemberRemovalError` with its step; after the last statement the step is `commit`, "saving the removal", so that a commit that fails is not named as the last statement's. Tests hook into the steps through the repository's third argument, `RemovalHooks.step`). Then, after the commit, what is outside the database: `LiveSignals.endSession` for each web login deleted (as a logout; wired late in `src/app.ts`, the hub being made after the context), `ModerationReports.forgetReporter` (`heldReports`: the reasons of reports held in memory after a failed post, `moderation.md`, which would write their id and name back with the next report of the subject), and `ScreenshotStore.forget`. None of them fails the removal, nor does the look at what is left that follows (`MemberRemovalOutcome.after`): one that throws is logged at warn, the outcome goes without it, and the answer says what went and to use the command again to see what is left; a removal that committed is never answered as a failure: the look and the answer stay within `FORGET_TEXT_BUDGET` (`src/render/member-removal.ts`, 3,500 characters, under Discord's 4,000), what is taken said whole, the hand-work and then what stays folded where they would cross it ("…and 2 more lines that do not fit in this message."), and an answer Discord refuses all the same is logged at warn (`removed a member’s data; the answer could not be sent`, with `member`), never thrown. One log line at info, `removed a member’s data on request`, with `member` and `by` and nothing else; a refused one at error with the step.

- **"Decided and over"** (`REQUEST_OVER`, `APPEAL_OVER`, `ASK_OVER` in `meetup-safety-repository.ts`): a request refused, expired, failed or withdrawn, or approved with its meetup made (`partyId`); an appeal lifted, kept or expired; an ask allowed, kept, expired or undone. Open, and left with the id and name: a request waiting for a moderator, an open appeal or ask, and a status the code does not know (the answer: have the moderators decide it, then run this again); and, counted apart (`MeetupRecordsHeld.unmade`, `REQUEST_UNMADE`), an approval whose meetup is still to be made, which no moderator can decide again (it is made with the maker's name and team within minutes; the answer: run this again once the meetup is made); and, counted apart as well (`MeetupRecordsHeld.live`), an approval whose meetup is still going on, which `REQUEST_OVER` alone would call over: the moderators are told of that meetup's changes through it (`approvedRequestOf`, `MeetupSafety.tellChange`), so it keeps its maker's id, name and team until the meetup has ended (the answer: the meetup is still going on; run this again once it has ended). Which meetups are going on is known to the service alone: `MemberRemoval` passes the ids of the meetups going on that the member made (`parties.active()`, as the look counts their live parties) to `MemberData.held` and `.remove`, down to `heldOf` and `forgetMember` (`going`); with none, everything `REQUEST_OVER` names is over. A meetup made from an approval in the moment between that read and the removal's transaction is not in the list and is cleared (accepted: a window of milliseconds).
- **The four ids the moderators' records kept** (`ModerationReportReason.reporterId`, `MeetupRequest.makerId`, `MeetupAppeal.memberId`, `MeetupPlaceAsk.memberId`) take a null since migration 051 (Matt's ruling of 2026-11-10, for this removal and nowhere else: the one change of Pine's own columns that is not additive, on tables the legacy bot never had). The reasons got a row id of their own as primary key, and `(reportId, reporterId)` stays unique, several forgotten reporters of one report standing side by side. Every reader takes a row without its member: a post or a list says "a former member" (`memberTextOrFormer`, `src/render/text.ts`); Block member on an old request's post whose maker is gone answers that there is nobody to block (`maker-forgotten`) and draws that post anew, with "a former member" and no Block member (the removal itself edits no post); no DM goes to a maker or member who is gone. The lookups by a member's id (`lastKept`, `approvedRequestOf`, "You already reported this.") simply no longer find the cleared rows: a forgotten member starts anew (they may appeal again at once, and report again what they reported), while their blocks stay. A meetup of theirs still going on keeps its approval with their id (above), so the moderators are still told of a change of its name, information or times.
- **The words that stay without who wrote them** are said in one line of the answer, counted before the ids go (the look's counts; after the removal its own, since the look after it no longer finds them by the member): their reasons on reports, and the words of their meetup requests (name, information, place), appeals and asks that are decided and over. What a report quotes of the member (the copy of a message they wrote, the meetup they made) is a report about them and keeps their id, as the line of those says.
- **Going back a version:** a version of Pine older than migration 051 reads these four columns as never null, which holds until somebody was forgotten. To run one, first remove 051's row from `kysely_migration` (`operations.md`, "Going back a version"; the columns stay as they are, which the older code does not mind until it meets a null).
- **What the owner is told to do by hand:** the member's files of the old bot's history, where `LEGACY_INDEX_DIR` is set and the member index holds any (`memberIndexServers`, `src/ops/legacy-index.ts`: `<server>/<member>.json` per server, the first `FILES_NAMED` (6) by their paths and the rest as "the file of that name in N more server folders there", so that the answer stays a message Discord takes; the bot's mount is read-only); backups, which hold the data until they age out (`BACKUP_KEEP_DAYS`); where `/request` is set up (`GITHUB_TOKEN` and `GITHUB_REPO`), the gym requests they sent, which are public issues of that repository under the name they had (`ByHand.requestsRepo`; Pine keeps no record of who sent one, so the line is said whenever the command is offered); posts on Discord, which are not edited; where `LEGACY_PARTIES_DIR` is set, the old bot's own `parties/` folder on the host, which holds members' ids as that bot wrote them (read-only to the bot: the owner edits those files); and, always, the bot's logs, which can hold an id and roll over by themselves (`ByHand.legacyParties`, `byHandOf` in `forget.ts`).
- **In memory** nothing is cleared but the remembered row id and their report reasons held after a failed post (`forgetReporter`, above): the member directory's and the names' answers and the boards' reads are kept a minute, a throttle's window its length, a login link `/maps` gave until it is used or expires, a direct message already in line until it is sent, a list-tier observation of a screenshot (`ListTierWatch`) a day.
- Tests: the repositories' methods each in their own `*.db.test.ts`, with a second member whose rows stay; `src/services/member-removal.db.test.ts` runs the real service over MySQL, a screenshot store and a member index in temporary folders, and compares a dump of every table before and after (the member's rows gone or blanked exactly where the table above says, every other row of every table as it was; a step made to fail leaves the dump unchanged). The command is tested over `memberRemovalWith()` (`testing.md`).

## Looking members up

- One member of a server is looked up with `ctx.members.find(guildId, userId)` (`MemberDirectory`, `src/discord/member-directory.ts`): a fresh question to Discord per member, the answer (also "not a member") kept for a minute; an id that is no snowflake is never asked about. `fetchGuildMember` is the one place a member is fetched (`DiscordPartyGateway.memberAccess` uses it; so do the web pages' `DiscordRegionStaff` and `DiscordWebMembers`, fresh). A member's name and team are read by `memberInfo(interaction)` from an interaction and by `guildMemberInfo(member)` from a fetched member (a press on the live map), by the same rule. Tests use `FakeMemberDirectory` (`test/support/members.ts`). There is no member list and there will be none: production has no privileged intents.
- Who of many members is in a server and can see a channel is `ctx.members.viewers(guildId, channelId, userIds)`: batches of 99 (and the bot itself, whose absence from an answer means the answer is no answer) through the gateway's request for members by user id, one request a second and thirty a minute at most (`MEMBER_REQUEST_BUDGET`), single REST look-ups when it fails and a rest that doubles up to six hours (`GatewayWithFallback`); "sees" is kept ten minutes, "does not" one (`src/discord/member-viewers.ts`). `FakeMemberDirectory` has `viewers`, `holding`, and members who are not there or cannot see.
- The names of many members (the boards', plan 8H-6) are `ctx.members.named(guildId, userIds)`: every id in batches of 99 by user id over the gateway (allowed without the GuildMembers intent; Discord's docs name the intent only for the whole list), never from discord.js's cache, which without that intent never hears that a member left (review A2); only while the gateway rests after a failure are the cache's members taken, the rest asked over REST one by one. Its requests and those of `viewers` go through one line (`BudgetLine.run`, A7, R-A4): one request for members at a time, the next after the last has settled and on one `MEMBER_REQUEST_BUDGET`; the notifier's (`viewers`) go ahead of names waiting (each turn chosen after the budget's turn is taken, a turn of the event loop later, so that the next batch of a lookup chained on the one just settled is in line first, F2), and names may start `NAMES_PER_MINUTE` (10) times a minute by the directory's clock (F4), a share of the line's thirty, all the batches of an ask reserved at once or none (`reserveNames`, F1), beyond which `named` rejects at once, spending nothing (`MembersBusy`; W1: a busy history page never holds back the DMs of a raid). The web then answers 503, `/stats boards` its words for Discord being busy. A server the cache lacks rejects (A8): nothing is kept of it. Somebody not in the server is not among them. `ctx.memberNames` (`MemberNames`, `src/services/member-names.ts`) keeps each answer a minute (`MEMBER_NAMES_FRESH_MS`, "not in the server" too; a failure is not kept), lets a second caller wait for a name already on its way rather than ask again (B4), and gives only members with a name, so that a board leaves out the rest and its ranks close up (never a mention in their place). `FakeMemberDirectory.named` answers from its members and records each ask in `namedAsks`.

## Self-assigned roles

- Self-assignable roles are `ctx.selfRoles` (`SelfRoleService`, `src/services/self-role-service.ts`): `roster(guildId)` is the server's rows (the legacy tables `Role` and `Alias`, with the Discord role's id in `Role.roleSnowflake` and the groups of `RoleGroup`) resolved against the roles the server has now, each entry with its `problem` if it cannot be given; `change(guildId, member, request)` plans with the pure `planChange` (`src/domain/roles/self-roles.ts`) and carries the plan out one member at a time, give first, then take, re-reading the roster before every further step, and answers (`RoleChangeOutcome`) `unavailable` (the bot does not know the server's roles), `refused` (`busy`: three changes of that member already wait; `expired`: the interaction can no longer be answered), `done` (the plan carried out; it may have been empty, and a role it skipped is in `plan.skipped`) or `partial` (what was given, taken, failed and not tried). A member still in the server's screening is refused (`requireJoined`). A handler tells the member exactly that (`src/commands/roles/words.ts`: `gaveWords` and `tookWords` say what a change of one role did, for `/iam`, `/iamnot` and the web map). A change that took a role away is passed on to `onRolesTaken`'s listeners whoever asked for it (plan 8M-20, W2: `app.ts` has `AccessService.memberChanged` settle the forums of the member's parties). `change` has a second surface since plan 8M-20: the web map's "Your regions…" (`POST /s/<guild>/api/regions/<region>/join|leave`, `web.md`, "Your regions on the map"), whose `RoleMember` is the member fetched afresh (`held` their roles, `seenAt` when Discord answered), counted in the window of the role menus; which roles open a region is `regionChoices` (`src/domain/roles/region-roles.ts`) over the gateway's `viewsWith`. `addRole`, `removeRole`, `saveGroup`, `removeGroup` are staff's; each change they make moves `generation` on (plan 8Z-2, F-B10). The roster has a third reader: the map's public view, which holds a region one role of the roster opens by itself (`regionChoices` for somebody holding no role, `publicRegions`; `web.md`, "Two views, by who asks") and is decided again when `generation` moves.
- The bot never writes a member's role list. `RoleGateway` (`src/discord/role-gateway.ts`) has `server(guildId)` (the roles from the cache, the bot's standing) and `giveRole` / `takeRole`, one role per request: a list built from what an interaction showed would undo what anybody else changed meanwhile. Only the id of a current roster entry without a problem reaches `giveRole`. Tests use `FakeRoleGateway` and `selfRolesWith()` (`test/support/roles.ts`), `MemorySelfRoles` (`test/support/self-roles.ts`); the fake interactions take `roleIds` and the member's server `permissions`.
- What may be self-assigned is `roleProblem` (never `@everyone`, a managed role, a role at or above the bot's highest, or one that adds a permission of `MODERATION_PERMISSIONS` to what `@everyone` has, by itself or through a channel overwrite, or one whose name makes its holder staff of this bot: `addedModeration`, `channelModeration`, `src/discord/role-permissions.ts`; `isStaffRoleName`, `guards.ts`), checked when staff add a role and every time one is given; staff may offer only what they could give by hand (`offerProblem`: the owner, or Manage Roles on the server, read from `interaction.member.permissions`, never the channel's `memberPermissions`, and a role below their own highest). A new permission flag of discord.js must be sorted into moderation or harmless (a test fails otherwise).
- A legacy `Role` row is linked to its Discord role the first time it is resolved (exactly one unclaimed role of that name, without case), and its `roleName` follows a renamed role so the legacy bot keeps finding it (`buildRoster` says what to write, `SelfRoleRepository.remember` writes it, also during autocomplete). A group is pick-one or not; roles without a group are "Other".
- `/iam` without a role shows menus (`roleMenus`, `menuPages`, `menuRequest` in `src/domain/roles/menus.ts`; `renderRoleMenus`; the handler `roleControls` in `src/commands/roles/menus.ts`): one per group, a group over 25 split into ranges, five on a message or four with Previous / Next; custom ids `roles:<group row id|other>:pick:<part>:<page>` and `roles:page:prev|next:<page>`. The message is one components-v2 container of `LIST_COLOUR` (plan 8B-4): `### Your roles on this server` and a line of help, each menu under `**<group>** · pick one|pick any number`, "Page 2 of 3" with Previous / Next, and what the last change did in small print at the end. A menu gives only what it showed unticked and is now ticked, and takes away only what it showed ticked and is now unticked, read from the pressed message (inside its container, or in the rows of a message from before 8B-4); a pick-one menu asks for a take only with a "None" that was not ticked already. After every press the message is made anew from the roster and the roles the member holds now (`menusNow`), never copied, and shown through `showInPlace` (`OLD_MENUS` on a plain-text message of menus, the new ones privately below it); without the roster the answer is the sentence and "Use `/iam` again for the menus.". Nothing about an open menu is kept in memory.
