# The web server and the map

The HTTP server in the bot (`src/web/`, plans 8M-1 to 8M-6; spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-web-map-region-editor-design.md`): its settings and secrets, how it is built, started and stopped, sessions and CSRF, logging in with Discord and with the links of `/maps` and `/region edit`, rate limits, the public map's data and page, the parties on the map and joining them, reporting and changing raids on the map, reporting wild Pokémon on the map, starting, arranging and moving trains on the map, making and changing meetups on the map, max battles on the map (the server side, plan 8X-5; the page, plan 8M-9A), the Report button (plan 8M-9A), reports at stops on the map (the server side, plan 8R-3; the page, plan 8M-9B), the region editor, the Content Security Policy, and the rules for adding a route. Read before changing anything under `src/web/` or adding a page or an address. How an operator sets it up is `docs/web-setup.md`.

## Settings, secrets, start and stop

- Off unless `settings.web` is set (`src/config/settings.ts`, strict): `port` (required), `host` (default `127.0.0.1`; `0.0.0.0` in a container), `publicUrl` (the address every link points at: `https:`, or `http:` only for `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]`; the root of its host, kept without the slash: append a path as it is), `trustProxy` (default false), `tiles` (plan 8M-7: `stadia`, the default, Stadia Maps' Alidade Smooth with OpenStreetMap's to fall back to; `osm`, OpenStreetMap's alone; or `google`, Google's beside Stadia's; no key for Stadia: it checks the page's domain, `localhost` passes; `docs/web-setup.md`), `memberRole` (a role's name; blank or absent is unset: see "Who sees what"), `invites` (plan 8M-11B: a server's id to the invite its admin chose, `https://discord.gg/<code>` or `https://discord.com/invite/<code>`, a vanity code too, letters, digits and hyphens and nothing after it, `INVITE_ADDRESS`; a blank one is none, absent none for any server; see "The fix pass: trains and clusters"). An invalid `web` object stops the start like any setting. Pine serves plain HTTP; TLS and the way in (a tunnel, a proxy) are the operator's.
- Secrets are in the environment, blank is unset: `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET` (without it there is no "Log in with Discord"; the links of `/maps` work) and `GOOGLE_MAPS_BROWSER_KEY` (Google's tiles; without it `tiles: 'google'` shows Stadia's, `shownTiles`, `src/web/headers.ts`). `configWarnings` says both at the start. The browser key reaches the page (it is restricted by referrer); `GOOGLE_API_KEY` never does.
- `buildWebServer(deps)` (`src/web/server.ts`) builds the Fastify instance from what it is given explicitly (`WebDeps`: settings, the picture origins, the browser key, the servers the bot is in and those with a map (`serversWithMap`, plan 8M-14), the member directory, `Sessions`, the OAuth gateway or undefined, `LoginLinks`, `GeoCache`, the bundle, the region editor's `EditorDeps`, the live map's parties `PartyWebDeps`, its raids `RaidWebDeps`, its spawns `SpawnWebDeps`, its trains `TrainWebDeps`, its meetups `MeetupWebDeps`, its max battles `MaxWebDeps`, its reports at stops `StopWebDeps`, the application emoji's pictures `emoji` (plan 8M-8A), a clock, the logger); tests ask it through `inject`. `startWebServer` (`src/web/start.ts`) listens: a port it cannot take is logged at error and the bot runs on without its pages. `src/app.ts` builds the server after the login and starts it once the client is ready; the first shutdown step closes it, so no page starts a change while the bot is going: from that step on `EditorDeps.closing()` is true, and the editor's preview and save answer 503 "Pine is shutting down: try again in a few minutes.", also a save that was being checked when it began (`admin.save` is never started then).
- Fastify's own logger is off. Every request is logged at debug by `method`, its route pattern (`/auth/link/:guild/:token`, never the address it was asked with, which may carry a token or code), `status` and `ms`. Never log a token, cookie, OAuth code, CSRF token, secret or the query string of `/auth/*`; a failure is logged as `{ err }` with the route.
- Failures answer briefly (`sendFailure`, `src/web/answers.ts`): `{ error: '<a few words>' }` under `/s/<guild>/api/` (401 "Log in first.", 403, 404, 413, 429, 503 "Not available just now."), a short page otherwise, never a stack or what went wrong; 500s are logged. Every short page (`body.short`, `pages.css`: the failures, the top page, a login link's page, the answers' pages) is one card, the full 480 px where the screen allows, whatever it holds, narrower only on a narrow screen with its 16 px gutters (plan 8M-15, P1: `flex: 0 1 480px`; `src/web/short-page-card.test.ts`). Unknown addresses are 404 through the same handler. The body limit is 16 KB (`BODY_LIMIT`); a route that takes more (the editor's preview and save, 2 MB: `SAVE_BODY_LIMIT`) sets its own `bodyLimit`.

## Headers and the Content Security Policy

- Every answer carries `securityHeaders` (`src/web/headers.ts`, pure, tested): the CSP (`default-src 'none'`; scripts, styles and fonts from Pine alone, no inline style, and no inline script but the one of a login link's page, let in by its hash (`script-src 'self' 'sha256-…'`: `CONTINUE_SCRIPT_SOURCE`, the SHA-256 of `CONTINUE_SCRIPT`'s exact text, worked out once in `src/web/continue-script.ts`; 8M-7 review fix 6); pictures from Pine, `data:`, `https://tiles.stadiamaps.com` unless the tiles are OpenStreetMap's alone (plan 8M-7), `https://tile.openstreetmap.org` (always: the fallback), `https://tile.googleapis.com` with Google's tiles, and the picture hosts of posts (`pictureOrigins`: the origins of `POKEMON_ICON_BASE`, `MEGA_PORTRAIT_BASE`, the stops' `ENCOUNTER_PICTURE` (plan 8R-6; the megas' host) and `publicUrl`, whose `/eggs/` serves the eggs and `/pictures/` the stops' square pictures, never typed by hand); connections to Pine and, with Google's tiles, the Map Tiles API; `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `base-uri 'none'`, `form-action 'self'`), `nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY` and `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` (Google checks a browser key's referrer by Pine's origin, and Stadia the page's domain by it). Leaflet sets `style` attributes from its script, which `style-src` does not govern; a page that needs an inline style or another inline script is a design error (a change of `CONTINUE_SCRIPT` changes its hash with it; `login.test.ts` hashes the page's script and finds it in the header).

## Sessions and CSRF

- A session (`Sessions`, `src/web/sessions.ts`) is 32 random bytes in the cookie `__Host-pine_session` (`sessionCookieName`, `sessions.cookieName`; `sessionCookie`: HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax, path `/`, no Domain, 30 days). The `__Host-` prefix keeps a sibling host of the domain from setting or shadowing it (cookie tossing); a cookie of the plain name is not read then. Where `publicUrl` is plain http (this machine only) the browser keeps no Secure cookie: the name is plain `pine_session`, without Secure. The session is stored as the hex SHA-256 of the cookie in `WebSession` (migration 015; `WebSessionRepository`, its twin `MemoryWebSessions`; what a stored session is, `WebSession`, `WebSessionStore` and `SESSION_MS`, is in `src/web/session-model.ts`, which imports no Fastify, so that the repository does not depend on the web server). It says who the member is (`userId`, the name for the header), never what they may do. `sessions.of(request, reply)` reads the cookie once per request; a session last used an hour ago or more is renewed (row and cookie: 30 days from now). A login (`sessions.start(request, reply, user)`) first deletes the row of the session the request's cookie stands for, if any: a browser has one session, and the old cookie leaves no row behind. Logging out deletes the row and the cookie; the hourly upkeep (`PartyScheduler`, `webSessions`) deletes expired rows.
- What a member may do is asked per request, from fresh data (spec 2.5): `/api/me` asks `members.find(guild, user)` (kept a minute by the directory; a member who does not count on the map by `settings.web.memberRole` is none) and answers `{ loggedIn, name, member, csrf, readOnly, mayEditRegions }`, and `missingRole` (the name `settings.web.memberRole` gives, plan 8M-6) for a member of the server who does not count on the map for lack of that role (`countsOnTheMap`, judged on the directory's answer by the route itself), never for somebody not in the server: a member sees the server's roles on Discord, so naming it tells nothing a stranger could learn here; when Discord cannot say, `member: false, readOnly: true`, and the page says it is read-only. The header names the missing role on the map only (`headerLines(me, login, page)`, `src/web/client/notice.ts`): the region editor is for staff, whom the map's role does not concern. One login serves every server; membership is asked per server.
- Every POST runs `postGuard(sessions, publicUrl)` (`src/web/csrf.ts`) as its `preHandler`, or in `onRequest` where the route has a body (the region editor's, a party's press), so that a refused request is never parsed: `Origin` must equal `publicUrl` and `X-Pine-CSRF` must be the session's token (`csrfToken`: an HMAC-SHA256 of the session id under a key made at start, compared in constant time); anything else is 403. The one exception is the POST of a login link's page, which has no session yet: it checks `Origin` alone. GETs (and HEADs) change nothing.

## Logging in

- With Discord (only with `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET`; else both routes are 404): `/auth/login` sets `__Secure-pine_oauth` (`oauthCookieName`: plain `pine_oauth`, without Secure, on http of this machine; the state, 16 random bytes, and the page to return to; HttpOnly, path `/auth/`, ten minutes; `__Secure-` rather than `__Host-` because of its path) and sends the member to Discord's consent page for the scope `identify`; `/auth/callback` deletes that cookie whatever happens, checks the state in constant time, exchanges the code (`OAuthGateway`, `src/web/discord-oauth.ts`: `DiscordOAuth` over `fetch`, ten seconds, each answer read up to 64 KB with `readLimitedJson`, failures as a few words; `FakeOAuth` in tests), asks who the token is (`global_name ?? username`), starts a session and returns to the page. Any failure returns with `?login=failed`; the token is used once and never kept. A `code` that is no short plain token (`OAUTH_CODE`, `^[A-Za-z0-9._~-]{1,128}$`) never reaches Discord (plan 8Z); `DiscordOAuth` makes at most one exchange a second across the process, failing fast above that (`busy`, logged, no request), and a 429 on the exchange or on `/users/@me` stops both for its `Retry-After` (60 seconds when that says nothing readable). The limit lives inside it, so `FakeOAuth` knows none of it. The top page's "Log in with Discord" starts it with `next=/` (plan 8M-14), so a failed or declined login lands on `/?login=failed`, which says so; a state that does not match, or a cookie that is gone, returns to `/` as well (the callback's fallback).
- With a link of `/maps` or `/region edit` (`LoginLinks`, `src/web/login-links.ts`, on `ctx.loginLinks`): 32 random bytes, kept by their SHA-256 in a `BoundedMap` of 10,000, five minutes, once, for one member, one server and the page it opens (`LinkPage`: `map`, or `regions/edit`, whose address ends in `/edit`: an expired link is known no more, so the address says where `?login=expired` lands; a valid link opens its own page whatever address it came by), named as the account is (`globalName ?? username`, as the login with Discord names it: one login serves every server). `GET /auth/link/<guild>/<token>` (and HEAD) uses nothing up: it answers a short page (`buttonPage`, `src/web/answers.ts`) whose form POSTs to the same address, and whose one script, inline (`CONTINUE_SCRIPT`, `src/web/continue-script.ts`, let in by its hash: no file of its own, which was a third request to `/auth/` for every login), presses the button as the page is shown (Matt, 2026-10-25: no second tap): it submits with `requestSubmit`, or `submit` where a browser lacks it (Safari before 16), then disables the button and puts "Logging you in as <name>…" in place of the sentence, the name read as text from the form's `data-name` (escaped on the page; never HTML built of it), so that a tap while the POST is on its way cannot abort it after the link was spent and land on `?login=expired` (8M-7 review fix 1; `login.test.ts` runs the page's script against a small document of its form, button and sentence). A link preview or a prefetch runs no script, so it cannot spend the link; without script the button stays. The button names the account: "Continue as <name>" (`loginLinks.peek(token, now)`, which spends nothing; the name stored with the link, HTML-escaped). Pressed by the script, somebody else's link logs you in as them without a pause; the map's header names whose account it is (Matt's ruling, 2026-10-25). An unknown, used or expired link, or one of another server, leads (303) at once to the page its address names with `?login=expired`. `POST /auth/link/<guild>/<token>` (and `…/edit`) checks that `Origin` is `publicUrl` (else 403), uses the link, starts a session and redirects (303) to the link's own page without the token (`/s/<guild>/map`, with `?region=<id>` for a link of `/maps` used in a region's channel, plan 8M-7, or `/s/<guild>/regions/edit` for a link of `/region edit`, whatever address it came by, which leads staff on to the map with the editor on, plan 8M-10); an unknown, used or expired link (or one of another server) leads (303) to the page its address names with `?login=expired`: the map, or with `/edit` the region editor, so that an expired editor link lands on the editor. The guild is in the address so that an expired link still lands on its server's pages. The answers of `/maps` and `/region edit` say so under the button: "The button works once, within five minutes; do not share it: it logs in whoever opens it as you. Use /maps again for another." (Matt, 2026-10-18). The page's plain address is given only as code, after "To share the map, give its address:", which Discord does not make a link: tapped above the button, it opened the page logged out (Matt, 2026-10-25); the button is the one link in the answer. The auth routes take an `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` body (the form's, empty) and ignore it.

## Live updates on the map

Design `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-map-live-updates-design.md`. A stream says only that a list changed; the fetch goes through the existing routes and `PartyViewers`, so no domain fact is decided here. Polling at 20 seconds stays the normal path wherever the stream does not reach (a quick tunnel, a buffering proxy, a dropped connection). There is no setting to turn it off (Matt, 2026-10-31).

- **The stream.** `GET /s/<guild>/api/live` (`src/web/live-routes.ts`): `LIVE_PER_MINUTE` per address, the session (401), `viewers.askedMembership` (403, 503), each a normal answer before the stream opens. Sent by `reply.send(passThrough)`, never `reply.hijack()`, which skips the security headers and the cookie writer: a session renewed on the way keeps its cookie. `content-type: text/event-stream`, `cache-control: no-store, no-transform`, `x-accel-buffering: no`. The events: `event: changed`, `data: parties 4000` or `data: stop-reports 4000`, and `event: ping`, `data: 4000`, at once and every 25 seconds (`LIVE_PING_MS`), the number being the spread below in milliseconds. A ping carries data because `EventSource` drops an event without any and hides comments from the page. A stream lasts 30 minutes and up to 5 more at random: its reconnect asks the session and membership again.
- **The hub.** `LiveSignals` (`src/web/live-signals.ts`), pure over its timers, clock and random (`LIVE_ON_THE_CLOCK` in the bot). Caps in plain `Map`s deleted at zero: 8 streams per address, 4 per member (over all servers), 2,000 in all, a 429 beyond them; the checks and the registration are one turn. Per server and list one signal per window (`Coalescer`'s rule, kept here for its injected timers and growing window): the first after a quiet spell at once, later ones merged to the window's end, one second per 10 open streams of the server, 5 to 20 seconds. The spread goes with every signal and ping: one second per 20 open streams of the process (one process answers every server, whereas the window is per server), 1 to 20 seconds. As averages, the window holds a list's asks at about 10 a second up to 200 maps, and past that a map asks as often as it polls, so the average load is no worse than polling; the peak is what the spread limits, about 20 asks a second per list up to 400 maps while one server's signal goes out, the lists alone: the posts a list changed add to it inside the same spread (one ask of each list costs about 8 ms of the thread on a loaded laptop, without the database: 20 a second is about 16% of a thread, kept that low because real load is not known yet). The lag can be worse than polling at the top end (at 400 maps and more, a merged change can take the 20-second window plus the 20-second spread). A write refused by backpressure destroys the stream: a slow reader reconnects. A stream's end at its lifetime is polite (`sink.end()`); a slow reader and the shutdown destroy it (`preClose`, `LiveSignals.close`), since a stream ended there could leave its keep-alive socket after the server's `closeIdleConnections`. A request past the router after that answers 503. Fastify's `onResponse` does not fire for a dropped stream, so its `web request` line at debug is written when the stream closes.
- **The sources** (`liveSources`, `src/app-wiring.ts`). `PartyStore.onChange` marks an active party's change pending, since a popup draws the post by `postKey` and its edit is usually still under way; `PartyService.onPostShown` (from `keepLayout`) flushes it, or signals where `postKey(layout)` differs from the one kept; a timer of 1.5 seconds flushes it if no draw comes; an ended party signals at once. Rows are mapped to snowflakes only while a server has a stream. `StopReportListener.listChanged` (not `changed`, which is not told of grunts): research and Rocket reports, a grunt refined in place, a take-back, a hideout put back, and through `StopReportService.linksChanged` a grunt's message stored and a draw of "Today's Stops", whose links the view shows.
- **The page.** `LiveConnection` (`src/web/client/live.ts`): live from the first `ping` or `changed`, never from `open`, not live after 60 seconds of nothing, which closes the stream. Every `onerror` closes it too, or the browser's own retry would run beside the page's: 5 seconds doubling to 5 minutes, random across the whole of it and at least over the latest spread, starting over only after a second ping, and never within `LIVE_FLOOR_MS` (20 seconds) of the last opening; that wait holds across a hide and a show. While hidden or under the region editor the stream is closed and the state not live, synchronously. `LiveList` (one per list) sets `Poller.setEvery`: 2 minutes only while live, the last answer good and the list not `readOnly`, else 20 seconds. It asks once on going live (`Poller.askOnce`: a random wait inside the ping's spread, no sooner than the floor), at a signal (`Poller.signalled`: the same random wait; a second signal keeps the first's timer, and takes the place of going live's, so a change does not wait out the floor; a press's `now()` stays immediate) and at a party's next hatch or end (`nextPartyMoment`, the parties' list only; while not live the poll has it). Going not live never asks at once: `Poller.setEveryScattered` waits `random * max(spread, 20 s)`, since a restart drops every stream together; `askOnce` adds its random part on top of the floor for the same reason. The posts of a list a signal or going live caused (`load`'s `spreadMs`) are asked for inside the spread (`PostViews.prefetch`), what a popup or card shows at once; the first list of a visit and a press's list as before. A 401 or 403 found by polling (`PollerDeps.stopped`) closes the stream for good.
- **Member look-ups.** A signal makes every map ask, and answers that ran out would each be a forced request of Discord. `CachedMemberDirectory` keeps an answer 60 to 90 seconds, at random per entry. `PacedMembers` (`src/web/paced-members.ts`, built in `buildWebServer`, closed in `preClose`) queues what `PartyViewers.askedMembership` asks: `/api/parties`, a party's post, `/api/stop-reports` and `/api/live`, at most 20 fresh fetches a second (`MEMBER_LOOKUPS_PER_SECOND`) and one at a time, the next when the last has settled or after 5 seconds (`PACED_FETCH_PATIENCE_MS`), first in first out, one per member, over `holds` of the directory, so an answer kept passes at once. One at a time because discord.js runs one server's member requests one after the other: a pace of starts alone would move the backlog into its queue. `membership` and `members.find` (presses, `/api/me`, the forms' lists, an answer's view of a report made, Discord's handlers) do not wait in this queue, and wait behind at most one paced request; what they fetch releases whoever waited for the same member. Whoever waits at the shutdown is refused by `MembersClosingError`, unconfirmed with no warning. A list's question has a limit of its own (`LIST_ASK_LIMIT_MS`, 130 seconds, past the queue's accepted tail), ending in the normal failed answer. `memberAccess` of a forum is not queued: it reads what the fresh fetch left in discord.js's cache.
- **What an ask costs.** Parties that did not change cost little (design, "What an ask of the parties costs"): `BossFeedService.bosses` gives the very same list until it changes (raids only, `lastsUntilOverridesEnd`); `PartyService.raidLookOf` keeps a raid's look in a `WeakMap` on the party object, compared at each ask with its stage, gym's row, boss list and whether the eggs are served; `countsOf` (`party-view.ts`) keeps a party's counts and `newcomersRegroup` (`party-routes.ts`) Group… for whoever is not signed up, by the same key. Whatever depends on the viewer or the clock is computed at each ask, and the answer is byte for byte an uncached build's (`party-view-cache.test.ts`). A new thing `raidLook` reads must join `raidLookOf`'s comparison. The stop-report list keeps nothing: its rows are read anew at each ask, so nothing has an object to key by.
- **Redraws** come up to four times as often. `GroupPicks` (`parties.ts`) carries `select[data-group-pick]` across draws by party, since Join sends what the menu shows.
- **Left to other paths:** a report's end and a grunt's minutes the page draws itself (`atNextEnd`); research periods, event windows, `waiting`, a square picture ready, roles and `barred` (kept 60 seconds), `readOnly` flipping back, and a party whose row vanished wait for the 2-minute poll.
